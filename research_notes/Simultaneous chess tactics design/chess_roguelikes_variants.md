# Chess roguelikes and chess variants as design precedents for Chezz

Research date: 2026-10-09. Several pages (Wikipedia, Steam store) could not be fetched directly from this container (DNS failure), so most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages. Treat exact numbers (review counts, scores) as snapshots.

## Which chess roguelikes from 2020–2026 matter, and how were they received?

### Takeaway
There are three main precedents. Pawnbarian (2020–22) uses card-driven moves for a lone hero. Shotgun King (2022) is a lone king versus a full army, where enemy pieces have move timers. Gambonanza (May 2026) is a Balatro-like that sold 300k copies but got mixed critic reviews. Passant (Aug 2025) is a smaller rule-bending Balatro-like. Most other titles are small. Checkmate Showdown is really a fighting game.

### Cited Findings
**Shotgun King: The Final Checkmate (PUNKCAKE Délicieux, 2022)**
- © 2022 PUNKCAKE Délicieux / Headbang Club / Plug In Digital. On PC, Xbox, PlayStation and Switch. — [Epic store](https://store.epicgames.com/p/shotgun-king-android-74f556?lang=ja); [TheXboxHub](https://www.thexboxhub.com/?p=143961)
- Setup: the player is the lone black King with a shotgun and must kill the white King on each floor. A full run is 12 boards. The King cannot capture by moving onto a square. The gun holds 2 shells and reloads when you move. Its spread does heavy damage up close or light damage to several pieces at range, so enemy pieces have HP. — [TurnBasedLovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/); [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/)
- Each white piece has a speed rating: how many turns it waits before moving. Several pieces can move at once, and a piece about to act quivers or wobbles. — [TurnBasedLovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/); [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499/shotgun-king-switch-review)
- Souls: killing a non-pawn, non-king piece gives you its soul (you start with 1 slot). Spending it lets you move once like that piece, for example a knight leap to escape. — [StackUp](https://www.stackup.org/post/review-shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate)
- Upgrades come as paired cards between floors. A black card helps you and a white card buffs the enemy, for example an extra knight, a pawn spawned every 5 turns, or a moat on rank 5. There are about 55 of each. A strong black card can be paired with a white card that is "a death sentence". A Steam thread complains that one catch-22 pick can ruin a run. — [HardcoreGaming101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/shotgun-king/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1972440/discussions/0/3551679689850940322)
- Forgiveness: by default the game blocks up to 2 moves that would end your run (shield icons), and the shields refill after each escape. — [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499)
- Praise: it shows clearly who moves next. Timers let several pieces attack at once, so you have to plan ahead. Reviewers found "a surprising amount of depth" under a gimmick premise. — [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499); [Buried Treasure](https://buried-treasure.org/2022/07/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate/); [Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/?p=454)
- Criticism: steep learning curve, difficulty spikes, randomness that "disrupts the tactical elements", repetitive runs, and that it is "chess with only one piece". — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/); [StackUp](https://www.stackup.org/post/review-shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate)
- A chess-literate player on the Steam forum was confused by the game's own quirks (kickback, black-mist respawn, jumping over enemies) and found detection of fatal shots inconsistent. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1972440/discussions/0/3389534247567161384)
- An aggregator lists Steam sentiment as Very Positive (about 8/10). — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1972440/shotgun_king_the_final_checkmate/stats/details); [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/14838/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate/reviews)

**Pawnbarian (Jan Wojtecki / j4nw; 7DRL origin; PC and mobile, iOS 2022)**
- Started as a one-week 7DRL jam game. Made mostly solo by Jan Wojtecki. — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2022/06/14/pawnbarian-iphone-ipad-release-date/)
- A single hero moves by playing cards. You draw 3, each card is a chess piece's move, and enemies act once your moves are spent. Cards have traits and can be upgraded in a shop. Playable characters have passives, and some add ranged attacks. — [Gideon's Gaming](https://gideonsgaming.com/pawnbarian-review-working-knights-in-a-dungeon/); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/pawnbarian/mobile-review/); [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/games/review-pawnbarian)
- Clearing faster earns more gold, which rewards efficient lines. — [Gideon's Gaming](https://gideonsgaming.com/pawnbarian-review-working-knights-in-a-dungeon/)
- Reception: "A tiny and mighty game" (RPS blurb). "A minimalist roguelike that's hard to put down" (Pocket Gamer). "Easy to get into, difficult to master" (GamingOnLinux). A "no-nonsense delight" for tactics fans (TapSmart). — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/pawnbarian/mobile-review/); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/articles/19844/); [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/games/review-pawnbarian)
- Some enemies die in one hit and others have gimmicks (an octopus multiplies). — [TapTap review](https://www.taptap.io/th/post/1563064)

**Gambonanza (Blukulélé, 1 May 2026)**
- The goal is to capture every enemy piece, not to checkmate. The board starts tiny (about 4×6). If you stall between captures, tiles crumble. Lost pieces are gone for good, so you buy replacements. Placing a new piece uses your action for the turn. Over 150 "Gambits" (Balatro-style modifiers), with up to 5 active. Enemy traits include Elite (must be captured last) and Crumbler. Every 5 matches there is a boss inspired by a grandmaster. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambonanza); [Pocket Gamer review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/gambonanza/review/); [Rogueliker](https://rogueliker.com/gambonanza-review/); [Checkpoint Gaming](https://checkpointgaming.net/reviews/2026/05/gambonanza-review-checkmate/)
- Commercial result: the demo was downloaded more than 170k times. It sold 200k+ in two weeks and 300k in three months (about 135k on Steam). The developer netted about $220k. Their two previous games sold fewer than 700 copies combined. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambonanza); [Niche Gamer](https://nichegamer.com/gambonanza-sells-300000-copies-across-steam-and-mobile/)
- Critics: Metacritic 64 (PC), OpenCritic 57% recommend. Reviews ranged from Destructoid 4/10 to DualShockers 9/10. Steam: Mostly Positive (about 1.2k reviews). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambonanza); [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/?p=1211574)
- Criticism: the AI makes random or suboptimal moves. About half of the gambits feel useless depending on your army. Fixed enemy setups per stage. Too much luck. The stalemate rule combined with crumble mode frustrated one reviewer. Praise: once you learn it, it becomes "a deep and surprisingly intense loop". — [Rogueliker](https://rogueliker.com/gambonanza-review/); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3509230/gambonanza); [Boiling Steam](https://boilingsteam.com/gambonanza-review/); [Entertainium](https://entertainium.co/?p=22131)

**Passant: A Chess Roguelike (11 Aug 2025)**
- A rule-bending chess roguelike with fairy pieces (Camel, Buffalo) and twists such as Atomic captures, Reflecting Bishops and Zombie Pawns. Its UI, turn system and shop are widely described as very close to Balatro's. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/passant/steam-launch); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3353100/discussions/0/604146775975003760)
- In the early demo (Feb 2025), a Camel placed on E2 could force a one-move smothered mate "nearly every time". The developer locked the Camel and Buffalo to the first rank of the setup board. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3353100/discussions/0/604146775975003760); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Chess-meets-roguelike-New-Steam-demo-takes-the-top-spot-in-Trending-Free.955541.0.html)
- About 79% positive of roughly 367 reviews (aggregator snapshot); another snapshot shows Very Positive (364). — [gg.deals list](https://gg.deals/game/roguelike-chess/similar-games/); [steamdata.ai](https://steamdata.ai/game/3353100)

**Smaller or adjacent titles**
- Yes, My Queen (Nov 2025, card power-ups, 82% of 50 reviews). Roguelike Chess (Feb 2024, Early Access, you control one piece and gain skills from cards). ChessRogue (Oct 2024, Early Access). Hyper Chess (Aug 2025, real time). King of the Bridge (Mar 2024, you bend a troll's rules). — [gg.deals](https://gg.deals/game/roguelike-chess/similar-games/); [Steam: Roguelike Chess](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2792900/Roguelike_Chess/); [wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/games/chess/)
- Checkmate Showdown (BadRez, Nov 15 2023): attacking a piece starts a fighting-game duel, and each piece is a fighter archetype. Very Positive at about 185–237 reviews, mostly an online versus game. Not a tactics precedent. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1803410); [gg.deals](https://gg.deals/game/checkmate-showdown/)
- itch.io has a long tail of chess roguelike jam games. — [itch.io tag](https://itch.io/games/tag-chess/tag-roguelike)
- Really Bad Chess (Zach Gage, 2016, pre-window but foundational): random armies, not mirrored. Pure randomness "doesn't feel like a game", so generation follows light rules (only B, N, P and rarely R in the front row; king always on its normal square). The army mix depends on your rank: early on you get queens and rooks while the AI gets minor pieces, and the AI gets stronger as you climb. It is faster and more aggressive than chess. — [Vice/Motherboard](https://motherboard.vice.com/read/really-bad-chess-is-a-really-fun-insult-to-chess); [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/?p=204079); [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20161018-really-bad-chess); [MetaFilter quoting Gage](https://metafilter.com/162828/Because-what-Ive-really-done-is-Ive-ripped-out-the-core-of-chess)

### Inferences
- The best-reviewed games (Pawnbarian, Shotgun King) keep the player's force tiny, one hero, and use hand-made enemy behaviour. The Balatro-likes (Gambonanza, Passant) sell well but draw criticism for luck and for weak or erratic opponent AI. For a tactics game, the opponent's quality and predictability is the main weak point.
- "Below the Crown", "Chessmancer" and "Royal Chess" did not appear in any search, so they are probably not notable releases.

## What does each game change about chess?

### Takeaway
They split along five levers: who has the army (lone hero vs. army), how moves are chosen (cards vs. free choice), when enemies act (timers and telegraphs vs. alternating turns), how pieces die (HP vs. capture), and what is won (kill the king vs. capture all vs. survive).

### Cited Findings
- HP vs. capture: Shotgun King gives enemies HP, and the shotgun's spread spends damage across the board. The player-king dies to any capture, softened by up to 2 blocked fatal moves. — [TurnBasedLovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/); [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499)
- Asymmetric sides: Shotgun King is 1 vs. many. Really Bad Chess uses random, unequal armies on purpose. Gambonanza builds a persistent army that loses pieces for good. — sources above
- Card-driven moves: in Pawnbarian, the cards drawn decide which piece moves you have each turn. — [Gideon's Gaming](https://gideonsgaming.com/pawnbarian-review-working-knights-in-a-dungeon/)
- Enemy timing: in Shotgun King, each piece waits N turns and then acts, wobbling first. — [TurnBasedLovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/)
- Board and win conditions: Gambonanza has a 4×6 crumbling board where you must capture every piece. Shotgun King adds terrain via white cards (moat). — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/gambonanza/review/); [HardcoreGaming101](https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/shotgun-king/)
- Recruitment and upgrades: Shotgun King uses paired help/harm cards and stolen souls (one-shot piece moves). Gambonanza buys pieces and Gambits. Pawnbarian upgrades cards. — sources above

### Inferences
- Chezz's "exiled king recruits pieces" sits between Shotgun King (the king is the hero) and Gambonanza (a persistent army with permanent loss). Shotgun King's souls (move once like a piece you defeated) fit an exile theme well as a lighter recruitment form.
- Shotgun King's paired black/white cards are a clear way to make each upgrade also raise the next encounter's difficulty. Players dislike it when a forced pair can end a run, so always offer a way out (skip or reroll).

### Gaps
- No source compared numerically how often card draws leave Pawnbarian players with no good move.

## What do players praise and criticize? Does chess knowledge stay relevant, and do chess tactics survive?

### Takeaway
Players praise readable threats (who moves next), tiny boards and short sessions. They criticize randomness that overrides planning, punishing upgrade pairs, erratic AI and gimmick rules that even chess players cannot read. Chess knowledge stays relevant when enemies move by real chess rules and threats are visible. It becomes irrelevant when luck or opaque special rules decide outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- Shotgun King "does an amazing job of showing who's moving next and giving the player chances to make a couple mistakes". — [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499)
- Timers let enemies attack together from several directions, so forward planning is needed. — [Buried Treasure](https://buried-treasure.org/2022/07/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate/)
- Randomness "disrupts the tactical elements" (Shotgun King). — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/pc-reviews/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/)
- Gambonanza players feel it "relies too heavily on luck". The AI makes random or suboptimal moves. — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3509230/gambonanza); [Rogueliker](https://rogueliker.com/gambonanza-review/)
- Passant's fairy pieces produced a degenerate forced mate on the first move until placement was restricted. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3353100/discussions/0/604146775975003760)
- Gage, on Really Bad Chess: "what I've really done is I've ripped out the core of chess". Its randomized armies remove opening knowledge, but tactics remain. — [MetaFilter](https://metafilter.com/162828/Because-what-Ive-really-done-is-Ive-ripped-out-the-core-of-chess)

### Inferences
- To keep the "aha" of forks, pins and discovered attacks, enemies must move like real chess pieces on deterministic, visible rules, and the player needs several pieces (a lone hero can't do a discovered attack). Chezz's recruited army can do this, which Shotgun King and Pawnbarian cannot.
- With HP, a fork only works if both forked pieces are actually threatened with a kill. Damage previews must show lethal vs. non-lethal so the player knows whether a fork is real.

### Gaps
- No review explicitly discussed forks or pins surviving in these games. That point is inference.

## What do chess variants teach about small boards, asymmetric starts and solved games?

### Takeaway
Very small boards can be solved: Gardner 5×5 is a weak-solved draw. Randomizing the setup (Chess960, Really Bad Chess) kills memorization while keeping tactics. Hidden information (Kriegspiel, Fog of War) is popular but changes the skill. Asymmetric armies can be balanced; engines can test this.

### Cited Findings
- Gardner 5×5 minichess was weakly solved as a draw by Mhalla and Prost (2013, ICGA Journal 36(4)). It has about 9×10^18 legal positions, comparable to checkers, and they solved it with surprisingly little computing power. — [arXiv 1307.7118](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.7118); [Wikipedia: Solving chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solving_chess)
- Los Alamos 6×6 (no bishops) was the first chess-like game played by a computer. I found no source saying it has been solved. — [Wikipedia: Minichess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minichess)
- Chess960 (Fischer, 1996; shuffle-chess idea from van Zuylen van Nijevelt, 1792): only the back rank is shuffled, under constraints (bishops on opposite colors, castling kept). The aim is to make opening memorization useless while general principles still apply. — [Wikipedia: Chess960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess960); [FIDE history](https://www.fide.com/history)
- Kriegspiel (1899) hides the opponent's pieces and needs an arbiter. Fog of War / Dark chess (1989) shows only the squares your own pieces can reach, has no check warnings, and is won by capturing the king. It was Chess.com's most popular variant in Sept 2020. — [Wikipedia: Dark chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_chess); [Chess.com club](https://www.chess.com/clubs/forum/view/fog-of-war-explanation)
- Atomic: a capture explodes neighbouring non-pawn pieces, and an exploded king loses. — [chessops docs](https://www.mintlify.com/niklasf/chessops/variants/atomic)
- Asymmetric armies: Orda (2020, Couch Tomato) uses a knight-based Horde army with a campmate win. Its variant site claims engine results near 50-50 (unverified). — [chessvariants.org](https://chessvariants.org/rules/orda-chess); [pychess](https://pychess-alternates.onrender.com/variants/orda)
- AlphaZero was used to assess 9 rule-changed chess variants. Piece values shift and some variants are more decisive, so self-play can test balance. — [arXiv 2009.04374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.04374)

### Inferences
- Small hand-built boards are fine for puzzle-like encounters because each encounter only has to be interesting, not unsolved. Chezz can brute-force-check small encounters for degenerate first-move wins (the Passant Camel lesson).
- Light generation rules (as in Really Bad Chess and Chess960) beat full randomness for varied boards.

### Gaps
- No balance data found for Horde or Monster chess. Monster chess searches returned nothing usable.

## Which games put HP or damage on chess pieces, and how did they keep it readable?

### Takeaway
HP chess exists mostly as hobby variants. The main open rules question is whether the attacker moves in when the target survives. Shotgun King is the only notable commercial example, and reviews credit its readability to telegraphing who moves next, not to damage numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Hit-point Chess (chessvariants.com): each piece has 30 HP. Damage is inverse to piece value (pawn 4, R/N/B 3, K 2, Q 1). A piece is removed at 0 HP. No en passant and no check; you lose with no king or no legal moves. — [chessvariants.com](https://www.chessvariants.com/invention/hit-point-chess)
- ChessToTheDeath (Python): per-piece HP and damage; you lose when your king's HP reaches 0. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ChessToTheDeath/0.0.7)
- In a chess.com forum proposal, HP equals piece value, the attacker also takes damage, and pieces heal on their home rank. — [chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/chess-variants/chess-variant-idea-pieces-with-a-health-bar)
- A BGDF thread flags the open question of whether the attacker advances when the defender survives. — [BGDF](https://www.bgdf.com/forum/archive/archive-game-creation/game-design/chess-game-using-more-pieces-health-points)
- Shotgun King shows intent by wobbling pieces and lets you undo fatal moves (2 shields). The shotgun's falloff (strong close, weak far) makes positioning matter. — [NintendoWorldReport](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/64499); [TurnBasedLovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/shotgun-king-the-final-checkmate-review/)
- Checkmate Showdown hands combat to a fighting minigame, so there is no HP readability problem to solve on the board. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1803410)

### Inferences
- High HP (30) makes captures take many hits and slows chess down. Small HP pools (1–4) with fixed damage keep kills countable at a glance. Chezz's "all hits use starting HP" snapshot rule naturally supports a per-turn lethal preview.
- Borrow: telegraph the next movers, a fatal-move guard, and lethal/non-lethal markers. Avoid: opaque special rules that chess players can't read (Shotgun King's kickback complaints), upgrade pairs that can be forced run-enders, AI that plays randomly, and luck-heavy shops.

### Gaps
- I found no source on how Shotgun King shows remaining HP or damage previews (numbers vs. pips).
- No commercial game was found that combines HP pieces, a player-controlled army and deterministic chess AI. This may be an open niche for Chezz, but I did not verify it exhaustively.
